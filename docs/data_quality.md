# Data quality control

The corpus is synthetic: sentences are generated by coding-agent CLIs with one
intended sense each, and a line is admitted only when the rule engine agrees with
the intended label. Because the rule engine *assigned* the labels, it cannot serve
as a self-check. QC therefore uses signals **independent** of the rule engine and
reviews only the lines where those signals disagree with the stored label —
far cheaper than reading the whole corpus, and where real errors concentrate.

## Method

1. **Multi-signal prediction.** Each line is independently re-labelled by signals
   that do not derive from the rule engine: a spaCy POS tagger mapped to sense
   (`pt_core_news_lg`), the Naive-Bayes model, and the averaged-perceptron model.
2. **Disagreement flagging.** A line is flagged when an independent signal
   contradicts the stored label. spaCy alone flags a large fraction (around a
   third), dominated by its own POS errors on minority (verb) readings, since the
   corpus is balanced 50/50 while spaCy is noun-biased. The high-precision signal
   is **both trained models disagreeing** with the label.
3. **Stratified adjudication.** A native-Portuguese LLM agent judges each flagged
   sentence's sense *blind to the stored label*. Adjudicate the full
   both-models-disagree stratum, plus random control samples from the spaCy-only
   bucket and the all-agree bucket to estimate baseline error rates.

## What the signal detects

The both-models-disagree stratum carries roughly 30% real-label-error precision
versus a ~1.3% baseline in the all-agree bucket — a large enrichment. Errors are
systematic rather than random and concentrate in a few confusable pairs, e.g.
`forma: shape↔mould` (baking-tin / casting *fôrma*, closed-o `ˈfoɾmɐ`, mislabelled
as the open-o "shape" reading), `sede: seat↔thirst`, and `molho: bundle↔sauce`.

Adjudicated disagreements are resolved by relabelling the line to the verified
sense, or dropping it when the target word is used in a sense outside the roster.

**Residual check.** After resolving the flagged set, re-adjudicate a fresh sample
of lines from the most-contaminated label that were *not* in the original sample.
A low residual contamination rate confirms the disagreement stratum had isolated
the systematic errors.

## Estimated corpus quality

With the model-flagged stratum cleaned, the bulk all-methods-agree lines and the
spaCy-only-flagged bucket each run around 98–99% correct, giving overall label
accuracy near 98.5% and the dominant systematic error class removed.

Reproduce: `qc_phase1.py` performs the flagging; the per-stratum sampling and agent
adjudication follow the procedure above and re-run on any corpus revision.

## Out-of-fold model signal (all words)

spaCy only helps POS-separable words and is noun-biased. For an independent signal
that **covers every word** — including same-POS pairs and words the shipped models
were not trained on — re-score the corpus **out-of-fold**: a 5-fold split per word,
each line predicted by NB+perceptron trained on the *other* folds. Because no line
is in its own training fold, the prediction is independent of that line's stored
label.

Both OOF models disagree with the label on roughly 0.5% of lines. Agent adjudication
of the flagged set (deduped against the spaCy/in-fold pass) detects the same
systematic sense confusions (e.g. `sede` thirst↔seat blocks, `forma` shape↔mould)
plus **generation-artifact lines** — agent chatter, file logs, and English prompt
echoes that leaked in as "sentences" (e.g. ``Ficheiro `frases_x.txt` com 40
frases``). Artifacts are identified by markers absent from natural sentences:
backticks, `.txt`/`.jsonl` references, "N frases", and "como verbo/substantivo".
Adjudicated lines are relabelled, dropped (defective), or swept (artifacts).

Reproduce: `oof_qc.py`.
