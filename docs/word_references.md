# bifonia — Word References

Per-word reference for European-Portuguese (pt-PT) heterophonic homographs: words
spelled identically across meanings but pronounced differently. The axis of
disambiguation for most pairs is open vs. closed stressed vowel. Each entry links to
the authoritative dictionary (Infopédia) and notes the disambiguation cue.

The disambiguation key is the **meaning** (`sense`); the `pos` column is the
descriptive grammatical reading of that sense and may repeat (both senses of `sede`
are nouns).

## Diacritic convention

The diacritic convention encodes vowel quality (acute = open, circumflex = closed):

| Diacritic | Vowel | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| acute (ó/é/á) | open vowel /ɔ/, /ɛ/, /a/ | pára (stop) |
| circumflex (ô/ê) | closed vowel /o/, /e/ | pêlo (hair) |
| no diacritic | default / ADP reading | para (to), pelo (by) |

## Diacritic-collapse & noun/noun pairs

These are written identically once the distinguishing accent is dropped in informal
text (acute = open, circumflex = closed). The first four are **noun/noun pairs** a POS
tagger cannot separate — they are resolved by meaning cues, joining the POS-ambiguous
set `{sede, molho, corte, forma, tola, bola, cor, lobo, polo}`. The last three are
deverbal noun/verb pairs.

| word | readings (open ɔ/ɛ · closed o/e) | resolver cue |
|---|---|---|
| bola | ball ˈbɔlɐ (*bóla*) · *bôla* bread/cake ˈbolɐ | baking/charcuterie context → loaf |
| cor | "de cor" = by heart ˈkɔɾ (*cór*) · colour ˈkoɾ (*côr*) | "de cor" + a knowing/reciting verb → by_heart |
| lobo | lobe ˈlɔbu (*lóbo*) · wolf ˈlobu (*lôbo*) | anatomical context (orelha, pulmão…) → lobe |
| polo | pole/sport ˈpɔlu (*pólo*) · young bird of prey ˈpolu (*pôlo*) | falconry context → fledgling |
| renovo | I renew ʁɨˈnɔvu (verb) · shoot/sprout ʁɨˈnovu (noun) | POS (deverbal) |
| soma | sums ˈsɔmɐ (verb) · sum/total ˈsomɐ (noun) | POS (deverbal) |
| força | forces ˈfɔɾsɐ (verb) · strength ˈfoɾsɐ (noun) | POS (deverbal) |

`bola`/`bôla` is the canonical example of the class.

## What makes a spelling pair heterophonic

A *noun (closed) / verb (open)* spelling pair is genuinely **heterophonic** when both
of these phonological conditions hold.

1. **Only -AR verbs open the stem vowel in the 1st person singular.** In European
   Portuguese the rhizotonic (stressed-stem) present forms of **-ar** verbs open the
   stem vowel (jogar → *jogo* /ˈʒɔɡu/), so the verb contrasts with the closed-vowel
   noun. **-er / -ir** verbs keep the 1sg **closed** (morrer → *morro* /ˈmoʁu/,
   identical to the noun; correr → *corro*; socorrer → *socorro*), so a deverbal noun
   paired with an **-er/-ir** verb has no contrast (e.g. *morro, socorro, escolho,
   corro, sorvo*). The infinitive-vs-noun pair *colher* is a separate, valid case.

2. **/e/ before a palatal (ʃ ʒ ʎ ɲ) diphthongises to [ɐj] in both readings, so there
   is no contrast.** *fecho* (noun) and *eu fecho* (verb) are both [ˈfɐjʃu]/[ˈfeʃu] —
   never *“fécho”*; infopedia marks these with the `(j)` glide. The -echo / -ejo /
   -elho / -enho families are therefore not heterophonic (e.g. *fecho, desejo,
   espelho, despejo, festejo, manejo, gracejo, arpejo, ensejo, desfecho, empenho,
   desempenho, desdenho*). **/o/ before a palatal opens normally** (olho /ˈoʎu/ vs
   /ˈɔʎu/), so *arrojo, jorro, despojo, abrolho* do contrast and are heterophonic.

Two further patterns produce no contrast: a noun whose stressed vowel is already
**open** (colo ˈkɔlu, cego ˈsɛɡu, prego ˈpɾɛɡu), and a stressed vowel that is **nasal**
(conto, ponto, desconto — õ has no open/closed contrast).
